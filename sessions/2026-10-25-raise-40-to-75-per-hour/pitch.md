# Your pitch: $40 to $75 an hour

Replace every [bracket] with a real number from your proof sheet. Do not say a number you cannot show. Meet the COO first, the CEO second. Aim for 10 minutes: 4 minutes of pitch, then silence.

## The beats (what you feel, what you say)

**1. Gratitude (warm, calm).** "Thank you for making time. I want to say first that I'm proud to work here and I want to keep building here for a long time."

**2. The story (proud, specific).** One real moment. "On [date], [system] handled [what] that used to take [people/hours]. Last week alone it saved [N] hours and [removed/earned] [$X]." Pick the moment that surprised you.

**3. The proof (steady, no adjectives).** Hand over the one-pager. "Three numbers, each from our own logs. Hours saved: [N] a week, about $[X] a year. Cost removed: $[Y] a year. Revenue lift: $[Z], where you agree on attribution. Together, about $[total] a year. [COO name] has checked this line."
Give the CEO the revenue line. Give the COO the cost and reliability line. Say that the core systems are documented and that [name] ran [system] from the runbook.

**4. The ask (confident, one breath).** "I'm asking for $75 an hour. I know that's a big step from $40, so here's how I'd get there with no risk to you: $55 now and $75 at day 90, against three targets we write and initial together today. Hit them, and the move is automatic."

**5. Stop talking.** Count to ten. Do not fill the silence. Do not apologize or add a reason.

## If they push back

- **"That's a big jump."** "It is, and that's why I'd tie it to numbers you've already checked. The 90 days costs you about $10k before you pay the full rate."
- **"We can't commit to $75."** Offer the fallback: "Then let's make it a base of $[60+] plus a bonus on [one metric you already report], capped, so I reach $75 only when it's earned."
- **"What if you leave?"** "That's why the runbooks exist. [Name] already ran [system] from them. I want to stay and keep building."
- **"Not now."** "Understood. Can we agree on a date to revisit, and on what numbers would change your answer?" Get a date in writing.
- Never say "or I'll leave." Decide privately whether $75 is a line you'd leave over.
